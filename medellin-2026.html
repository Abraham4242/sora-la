Chapter II02
Where to Live

Live in Estrato 5 or 6. Read the city by stratum.

Colombia uses a 1–6 socioeconomic stratification (estratos) that determines utility prices, neighborhood character, and — practically — your safety profile. Estrato 6 (top 5% of buildings) is suburban affluent (Poblado southwest, El Tesoro hill, Laureles core). Estrato 5 (the next 10%) is where you actually want to live — well-built, walkable, designed for upper-middle. Estratos 1–3 are the comunas where Medellín's complicated 1990s–2000s history lives. Don't pretend that's not a real distinction.

The Triangle

Laureles is the answer for 2026. Estrato 5–6, leafy, grid-laid (unusual for Medellín), tons of independent cafés (Hija Mía, Pergamino Laureles, Café Velvet), the new wave of restaurants (Mistura, Naan), and zero of the digital-nomad noise of Provenza. Rent is 30% below El Poblado for the same square footage. If you're new to Medellín, start here.

El Poblado is the corporate / family / "first time in Medellín" default. The southwest hill (around Parque del Poblado, Ciudad del Río, Manila) is genuinely beautiful — Pablo Atrio's modernist apartment buildings, walk to Carmen + El Cielo, easy access to the metro. Provenza specifically (the 8 blocks around Calle 8 + Cra. 35) is now a tourist trap with $14 cocktails. Live in Manila or the Castropol cluster, NOT Provenza itself.

El Tesoro is the high-end families' choice — the hill east of Poblado. CC El Tesoro mall, country clubs, $400k+ houses. Skip unless you have kids or want gym + grocery + restaurant within a 5-minute walk and don't care about street life.

The Long-Term Pick

Envigado is what mature Medellín expats migrate to after 18 months. Adjacent to Poblado south, Estrato 5–6, much quieter, better food market scene, and the colonial Parque Envigado on Sundays. Rent is 35% below Poblado.

Hard No-Go Zones

Comuna 13 (San Javier, 20 de Julio) is famous for the graffiti tour — a daytime guided 2-hour tour with a local artist is fine and worthwhile. Do not go solo, do not go after dark, do not stay overnight. Comuna 1 / Comuna 2 (northeast) and Bello commune (north) are off-limits without specific local sponsorship. Centro after 6pm has gotten better but is still rough — gallery visit in the day is OK, the Plaza Botero / Berrío stretch is the perimeter.

The Honest Recommendation

For Sora's launch base: Laureles around Avenida Nutibara + Circular 1, 2BR unfurnished apartment in a low-rise. $900–1,400/mo. 8-minute walk to Pergamino, 12 minutes to Mistura, 4 minutes to Parque de los Pies Descalzos. Sign for 12 months — Colombian leases penalize early termination but landlord turnover is high so negotiate the diplomatic-clause exit. By month 12, evaluate Envigado vs Manila.

Renting & Buying

Rent year 1. Buy only in Estrato 5–6 stock under $250k.

Medellín real estate has been on a 4-year run. El Poblado new construction is priced at $3,000–4,500/m² (Bogotá-comparable). The values are in Laureles + Envigado + Sabaneta where dollar buyers haven't fully arrived. Yields are 6–9% gross on furnished long-stay. Avoid: pre-construction promesa contracts with non-vetted developers, anything with title issues in Comuna 13 fringe, and Provenza-area buildings dependent on Airbnb supply that just compressed 18%.

Buying — What $250k Gets You

  • Laureles 2BR renovated, 75–95 m², parquet floors — $180–230k. Best total-return play in Medellín.
  • El Poblado Manila 2BR new build, 70–85 m², amenities + parking — $230–280k. Liquid market.
  • El Tesoro 3BR family apartment, 130 m², parking + bodega — $250–320k. Family demand, slower appreciation.
  • Envigado 3BR house with patio, 180 m² lot — $200–280k. The forever-home angle; harder to flip.
  • Provenza 1BR boutique, 50–70 m² — $220–280k. Be careful: this market just compressed. Buy only if you're long-Medellín-forever.

Buying Process — Compressed

  1. Pick a broker. Compass Real Estate Medellín, Casacol, or independent like Felipe Restrepo. Avoid Airbnb-owner-turned-broker.
  2. Get a Colombian RUT (tax ID). Via Brigard Urrutia in 1 week; $200. Required for property registration.
  3. Promesa de compraventa — 10% deposit + signed promise contract. Negotiable terms.
  4. Title study (estudio de títulos) via lawyer. 10 days. $500–800. Critical — Colombian title chains are messy; uncovered liens are common.
  5. Notarial deed (escritura). 30 days. Notary publica registers transfer.
  6. Registration at Oficina de Registro de Instrumentos Públicos. 15 days. ~1% of purchase as registration tax (boleta fiscal).
  7. Closing costs ~3.5% including notary, registration, lawyer, transfer tax (impuesto de beneficencia). Less than Mexico but real.
  8. Pay in COP at the notary. Convert USD via Buda.com or Bancolombia FX desk; close in pesos.
Sora Use-Case

The Sora-Medellín play: a Laureles 2BR ($200–230k purchase, $1,200/mo furnished rental income when not occupied). Net yield 5.5–6.5% in USD terms, plus 2–4% local appreciation, plus full availability for Sora hosts. Better economics than Mexico City; same logic.

Coworking density is second only to Mexico. Start at Selina.

Medellín has 60+ coworking options as of 2026, concentrated in El Poblado and Laureles. The post-Selina (it survived in MDE) and Atom House era. Start with a month-to-month, evaluate at month 4, upgrade to private suite or pivot to café+home if your work pattern is solo.

For Sora-MDE with 2–4 person team: $1,000–1,800/mo for a private 3-room office through IWG (Regus) or local broker. Better economics than CDMX. Most leases are 6-month terms, easy to break.

If you work mostly solo, the café network is enough. Pergamino Laureles + Hija Mía + Café Velvet are the wifi-stable rotation. Café Zeppelin (Manila) and Saudade (Envigado) for variety. Bring noise-canceling headphones — Colombian café culture is louder than U.S. (vallenato on speakers is normal).

Chapter VI06
Setting Up the SAS

SAS via attorney. Two weeks. $1,800 all-in.

Colombia's Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (SAS) is the easiest business setup in LATAM — period. No minimum capital, single shareholder allowed, foreign ownership 100% permitted, fully digital incorporation. Sora-Colombia is the chapter that should be operational fastest after Mexico.

Setup Sequence

  1. Hire counsel. Brigard Urrutia ($1,800 all-in, Tier-1) or Posse Herrera Ruiz ($1,200, mid-tier).
  2. Name search and reservation at Cámara de Comercio. 24 hours, $40.
  3. Draft statutes (estatutos). Counsel handles. 2–3 days. Define objeto social (business purpose) broadly to allow Sora's full vertical scope.
  4. Constitution and registration at Cámara de Comercio (Medellín). Online or in-person; 24 hours. Get your matrícula mercantil + RUT (DIAN tax ID).
  5. Bank account opening. Bancolombia, Davivienda, or BBVA. Bring matrícula + RUT + legal rep ID. 2 weeks; in-person required for foreign-owned SAS.
  6. Buda.com Business in parallel for USDC/COP. 10 days post-RUT.
  7. Optional: ICA (industry & commerce) registration at Alcaldía de Medellín. Required if you have a physical commercial activity in the city; not required for online + events.

Recommended Counsel

  • Brigard Urrutia — Tier-1 LATAM firm. English-speaking M&A team. Partner: Daniela Castaño. $1,800 incorporation + $250/mo retainer.
  • Posse Herrera Ruiz — Mid-tier, faster, less bureaucratic. $1,200 incorporation + $200/mo retainer.
  • Lloreda Camacho — Real estate specialty. Use for property transactions only.
  • NotaryColombia (online) — $400 simple SAS setup, no retainer. Acceptable for side entity that won't raise outside capital.

Hiring Local Staff — The Reality

Colombian labor law is moderate — less employee-favorable than Mexico, more than the U.S. Employer cost is ~1.52× gross (parafiscales: salud, pensión, ARL, cajas de compensación). For a $1,500/mo Sora-MDE host, total employer cost is ~$2,280/mo. Prima de servicios (mid-year 13th-month bonus, mandatory June + Dec) adds 16.6%. Cesantías (severance accrual) adds 8.3%. Vacation 4.2%.

Alternative: hire as contratista de prestación de servicios (independent contractor) — no parafiscales, no prima, no cesantías. Limit: contractor must control their own work + tools + schedule. DIAN audits this aggressively. PEO option: Deel or Ontop, ~12% on top of gross.

The "Best Setup in LATAM" Claim

Colombia's SAS really is the gold standard: zero capital minimum, fully digital, 14-day timeline, foreign-friendly. If you're choosing one LATAM country to incorporate in for the first time, Colombia is the right answer. Mexico (SAPI), Argentina (SA), and Brazil (Ltda) all take 4–8× longer and cost 3–5× more.

Chapter VII07
Visa & Residency

Migrante visa via Migración Colombia. Done from inside the country.

Colombia's visa structure is the inverse of Mexico's — you can apply from inside the country after entering as a tourist. The Visa M (Migrante) for "Rentista" or "Inversionista" pathway requires $9,400/mo income proof or a property purchase of ~$60k+ to qualify. Total: 4–6 weeks from inside Medellín, $400 in fees, no consulate trip required.

The Sequence That Works

  1. Enter Colombia as tourist. 90-day stamp on arrival (free, automatic for U.S. passports). Extendable to 180 days.
  2. Apply for Visa M online at the Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores portal within first 90 days. Choose pathway: Rentista (income), Inversionista (property), or Trabajador (employee of a Colombian company).
  3. Submit docs: passport + 6 months bank statements showing $9,400+/mo OR property purchase deed showing $60k+ OR Sora-Colombia employment contract, plus apostilled birth certificate (get it before you fly).
  4. $240 visa fee + $50 examination fee. Pay online.
  5. Approval in 5–15 business days. Visa stamped digitally to your passport.
  6. Cédula de Extranjería (foreigner ID card). Migración Colombia in El Poblado (Calle 19) — appointment online. $50, 2-week processing. This is the document you'll use for everything (bank, lease, mobile plan).

Three Visa Pathways

  • Rentista — most common. Proves you make $9,400+/mo from passive income, employment abroad, or freelance. 3-year visa, renewable. Best for remote workers.
  • Inversionista (real estate) — buy property valued at COP 350x current minimum wage (~$60k in 2026). Forever-renewable as long as you own. Best if you already plan to buy.
  • Trabajador — employee or legal rep of a Colombian company (e.g., Sora-Colombia SAS). 3-year visa. Best if you incorporate before applying.

Tax Residency — The 183-Day Rule

Colombia considers you a tax resident if you spend 183+ days in any 365-day rolling period in the country. This is independent of your visa status. A tourist who stays 183+ days is a Colombian tax resident. Same for Visa M holders. If you're going to be in Medellín > 6 months a year, plan for DIAN obligations.

Apostille Trap

You need an apostilled birth certificate to apply for Visa M. Apostilles must be issued in your birth state's Secretary of State office (or your federal Department of State for diplomatic births). This takes 3–6 weeks by mail. Start this before you fly, not after — otherwise your 90-day tourist stamp expires while you wait.

Chapter VIII08
Banking & COP Rails

Bancolombia for the bank, Buda for the rail, Daviplata for retail.

Colombian banking works but is paper-heavy. The crypto rails are surprisingly mature — Buda.com and Bitso both serve Colombia with regulated COP/USDC pairs. Daviplata (Davivienda's mobile wallet) is what street-level commerce runs on. For Sora-MDE, the stack is: Bancolombia + Buda + Daviplata for the three different jobs.

Banks — The Short List

  • Bancolombia — Largest, best app, best ATM network. Personal account opens in 2 visits (open, return for card) with cédula + RUT + proof of address. Corporate account 2–3 weeks. Default choice.
  • Davivienda — Solid #2. Owns Daviplata (the dominant mobile money). Better digital UX than Bancolombia. Pick if you want fewer in-person branch visits.
  • BBVA Colombia — Strong on international wires (better USD/EUR rates), worse domestic UX. Pick if you have Spain/Mexico ties.
  • Itaú Colombia — Brazilian, great private banking for $250k+ deposits, weak retail.

Crypto Rails — USDC, USDT, BTC

Buda.com is the answer. Chilean-headquartered, regulated in Colombia by SFC, native USDC and USDT pairs, wire in/out from Bancolombia or any Colombian bank. Spreads 0.5–0.8%. Open personal + Buda Business accounts.

Alternative: Bitso Colombia (Mexican company, expanding) — also regulated. Binance P2P COP/USDT works but is unregulated and has counterparty risk; don't use for large amounts.

Retail Rails — Daviplata, Nequi, PSE

Colombian street-level commerce runs on three rails:

  • Daviplata — Davivienda's wallet. Free transfers. Accepted at most small merchants. Get one day 1.
  • Nequi — Bancolombia's wallet. Same role. Slightly more popular than Daviplata.
  • PSE — Pagos Seguros en Línea. The Colombian online-payment rail used for utility payments, taxes, e-commerce. Comes free with your Bancolombia account.

The Money Flow That Works

  1. U.S. employer pays USD → U.S. bank.
  2. Wire USD → Buda.com (1–2 days, $25 wire fee).
  3. Convert USD → COP on Buda (0.5% spread).
  4. PSE from Buda → Bancolombia (instant, free).
  5. Spend COP via Bancolombia debit + Daviplata for retail.

For Sora-MDE revenue: guest pays USD → Stripe → Bitso/Buda → COP for Colombian ops. Net cost: 1.5–2% on cross-border flows.

Chapter IX09
DIAN & U.S. Tax

Cross 183 days, owe Colombia on worldwide income.

DIAN (Dirección de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales) is more aggressive than Mexico's SAT. The 183-day rule is enforced via immigration database cross-references, and Colombian tax residents owe income tax on worldwide income at progressive rates up to 39%. The U.S.-Colombia tax treaty doesn't exist yet (in negotiation since 2022). This is the biggest single risk of Medellín long-stay.

What You Owe / Must File

  • U.S. Form 1040 — every year regardless of residence. Worldwide income.
  • FEIE (Form 2555) — exclude up to $130k earned income if you pass bona fide residence or physical presence test.
  • FTC (Form 1116) — credit for Colombian tax paid. Critical because of treaty absence.
  • FBAR (FinCEN 114) — required if Colombian accounts > $10k aggregate at any point. Free, online.
  • FATCA (Form 8938) — if foreign assets > $200k single / $400k married.
  • Colombian DIAN declaration — annual, due August (varies by NIT last digits), if you cross Colombian tax residency.

The Sweet Spot Structure

  1. Stay U.S. tax resident. Maintain U.S. address, license, voter registration.
  2. Cap Colombian presence at < 183 days/year for the first 1–2 years. Track via Migration Colombia entry stamps. Spend remainder in Panama / U.S. / weekend trips.
  3. Sora-Colombia SAS is its own tax entity — corporate tax 35%. You're a shareholder, take distributions not salary.
  4. U.S. compensation (Chainlink Labs) stays paid to U.S. bank, U.S. taxed. FEIE eligible if qualified.
  5. SAS dividends — 7% withholding on distribution + Colombian recipient tax. Plan distributions to coincide with FTC availability.
The 183-Day Trap

If you cross 183 days in Colombia in any 12-month rolling period, you become a Colombian tax resident retroactively for that fiscal year — on worldwide income. The U.S.-Colombia tax treaty doesn't exist; the FTC can partially offset but the math gets brutal above ~$200k income. The honest play: keep Colombian presence < 180 days for years 1–2 while you evaluate. If you commit to Colombia permanently, accept the tax residency and restructure accordingly. Recommended counsel: Greenback Tax (US) + Brigard Urrutia tax team (Colombian).

Chapter XI11
Daily Safety Protocols

The bubble is real. Don't dar papaya.

"No dar papaya" is the Colombian phrase — don't give papaya, don't make yourself an easy target. Medellín is safer than Bogotá and much safer than its 1990s reputation, but it's not Mexico City. The risks are specific and avoidable: phone snatching, scopolamine (burundanga) drinks, fake police checkpoints, and Tinder/Bumble robberies in foreigner-targeting patterns.

The Habits Worth Keeping

  • Phone in pocket on the street. Medellín phone snatching is #1 reported foreigner crime. Motorcycle-borne. Phone in hand = target. Glance, then re-pocket.
  • Uber or InDriver only. Never flag a street taxi. Uber is technically illegal in Colombia but ubiquitous and safer; InDriver is the legal alternative.
  • Don't accept open drinks. Scopolamine (burundanga) is the Colombian roofie — odorless, tasteless, knocks you out for 24 hours during which you'll be robbed clean. Watch your bartender pour. Don't leave drinks unattended. Don't accept drinks from strangers in bars.
  • Tinder/Bumble vigilance. "Sex worker / accomplice" robbery pattern: match, meet at a bar, accomplices arrive, you're robbed. Always first meet in a public, well-trafficked Provenza or El Poblado venue you choose. Never meet at a hotel or Airbnb first.
  • Don't carry your passport. Cédula photo on phone is enough for everything but airport. Original passport in apartment safe.
  • No big cash withdrawals at standalone ATMs. Bancolombia or Davivienda inside branches only. Capped at COP 600k (~$150) per withdrawal anyway.
  • Avoid Comuna 13 + Centro at night. Daytime fine with guide; never solo after dark.
  • Fake police checkpoints exist. Real police never ask for cash. If stopped on the road by "police" demanding cash or to search your bag, drive to the nearest gas station and call 123.

What You Don't Need

No armed escort, no panic button, no varying routes. Medellín in 2026 is genuinely safe for normal habits. The risk is specific (phones, scopolamine, dating-app robbery) and avoidable.

Chapter XII12
First Ninety Days

The sequence that actually works.

Medellín's setup discipline is the lightest of any LATAM capital. The visa can be done from inside the country, the SAS in 14 days, the bank account in two visits. If you stay disciplined, you're fully operational in 60 days.

Before You Fly

  • Apostilled birth certificate — start this 6+ weeks ahead. Required for Visa M.
  • Bank statements ready — 6 months showing $9,400+/mo for Rentista, or proof of $60k+ for Inversionista pathway.
  • Counsel pre-engaged — email Brigard Urrutia or Posse Herrera Ruiz. Schedule kickoff call for week 1.
  • U.S. tax cleanup. Last year's 1040 in hand. 30-min consult with Greenback about FEIE / FBAR.
  • U.S. address solution. Earth Class Mail or family. Maintain U.S. license + voter registration.
  • USD liquidity. Move 4 months of burn ($14k) to U.S. account, ready to wire to Buda once Colombian bank opens.

Days 1–30 — Scouting

  • Land in Medellín on tourist stamp. 90 days automatic.
  • 10 nights at The Charlee Hotel or Click Clack. Hotel base for the first city tour.
  • Walk Laureles + Manila + El Tesoro + Envigado at 10am, 2pm, 8pm, 11pm — at least 6 times each. Match neighborhood to your actual rhythm.
  • Eat at Carmen, Mistura, Naan, El Cielo, Hatoviejo in first 3 weeks. Calibration matters.
  • Meet 5 expat operators via Expats in Medellín FB or InternationsMDE. Their first-year lessons save 3 months.
  • Choose your neighborhood + sign 12-month lease. Laureles unfurnished or El Poblado Manila furnished.
  • Apply for Visa M online within first 60 days.

Days 31–60 — Settling

  • Visa M approved. Cédula de Extranjería appointment booked.
  • RUT obtained via Brigard Urrutia. $200, 1 week.
  • Bancolombia account opened. 2 visits.
  • Buda.com personal opened in parallel.
  • Daviplata + Nequi on phone for retail.
  • Sora-Colombia SAS incorporation started with counsel. 14-day timeline.
  • Join Bodytech + Pergamino regular — the texture of daily life starts.

Days 61–90 — Operating

  • Cédula de Extranjería in hand.
  • SAS fully operational. Corporate Bancolombia + Buda Business open.
  • First Sora-MDE weekend hosted — soft launch with 10 guests at The Charlee Hotel.
  • Casa San Agustín (Cartagena) partnership in motion for Q1 2027 cross-chapter weekend.
  • MDE host hired via PEO. $1,500/mo USD all-in.
  • Decide: stay full-time Medellín (and accept > 183-day tax exposure), or quarterly base (and stay under). Honest review at day 90.
The 90-Day Decision

Medellín is the easiest LATAM chapter to launch and the easiest to stay in. By day 90, the question is whether you commit to Colombian tax residency or stay quarterly. If your annual income exceeds ~$200k, lean toward quarterly (keep < 180 days). If under, full-time Medellín pencils out well after the FTC math. Either way, the SAS + Casa Sansara partnership + MDE host run on their own — you don't need to be there to operate.
