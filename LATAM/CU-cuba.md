# SORA · Cuba Chapter

*Tier 3 · Politically gated · Target go-live: 2029+ (waiting for sanctions clarity)*

## Thesis

Cuba is the most aesthetically unique country in the Caribbean — Havana's faded grandeur, 1950s Detroit steel rolling through Vedado, the cigar-and-rum capital, Viñales tobacco valley, Trinidad colonial perfection. The opportunity is real but **operationally gated by US sanctions**. The chapter only opens once:

1. US Treasury OFAC restrictions ease materially (current US-person travel limited to 12 authorized categories, transactions with Cuban government entities prohibited), AND
2. A Spain or Mexico-based holding entity is set up to operate Cuba-side without US-person exposure

**Recommendation:** treat as a 2029-2030 target. Watch US-Cuba political indicators.

## Real estate strategy

**Foreign real estate ownership in Cuba is restricted.** Foreigners can buy in specific gated complexes (Cap Cana-style developments like Cayo Largo del Sur Resort), but the general residential market is closed to non-residents.

**Skip real estate as a product line in Cuba until laws change.** Focus the chapter entirely on travel + experiences + cultural programming.

---

## Legal entity + ops

**Cannot operate as a US-citizen-owned entity.** The Cuba chapter must run through a Spain or Mexico-based corporate structure, with the US-person founder having NO ownership stake (only a separate non-Cuba advisory role).

**Recommended setup:**
- Spain SL (Sociedad Limitada) owned by a non-US-person partner OR Mexico SAPI structured to avoid US-person economic interest
- Cuba operates as a representative office or commercial agent of the SL

**Legal counsel:** PWC Cuba advisory · Cuesta Morúa & Asociados (Havana-based) · Spanish-side counsel mandatory

**Bank:** No US-domiciled bank can process Cuba transactions. Spain or Canadian banks (Scotiabank Cuba branch) only.

**Hosts:** Cuban citizen as local lead (cuentapropista — self-employed). Foreign-staffed offices have restrictions.

## Risks (significant)

- **US-Cuba sanctions** — OFAC violations carry strict liability; any structural mistake destroys the entire Sora-LATAM holding
- **Cuban government overhead** — most tourism revenue currently captured by GAESA (military-owned conglomerate); avoid GAESA-affiliated hotels (most resorts)
- **Currency duality** complications (recently unified but volatile)
- **Internet + payment friction** — Cuba has limited internet; payment processing is the hardest in LATAM
- **Political shifts** in either direction can shut the chapter overnight

---

## Strategic role

Cuba is a **marquee chapter for brand prestige**, not for revenue. Have it when it makes sense (post-sanctions ease) as the most unique product in the Sora-LATAM portfolio. Until then, monitor and prepare.
